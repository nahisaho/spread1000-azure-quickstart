Fukuzawa Yukichi (1835–1901) was a Japanese author, educator, translator,
entrepreneur, and political theorist who is regarded as one of the most
important intellectuals of the Meiji era. Born into a lower-ranking samurai
family of the Nakatsu domain, he studied Rangaku in Osaka under Ogata Kōan
before shifting to English after realizing, on visiting Yokohama in 1859,
that Dutch was of limited use for engaging with the wider Western world.

Fukuzawa traveled to the United States twice (1860 and 1867) and to Europe
once (1862) as part of official Tokugawa embassies. His observations from
these journeys were published as Seiyō Jijō ("Conditions in the West",
1866–1870), which became a bestseller and shaped popular understanding of
Western political and economic systems.

He founded Keiō Gijuku, later Keio University, in 1858, one of the earliest
modern private universities in Japan. His most influential book, Gakumon no
Susume ("An Encouragement of Learning", 1872–1876), argued for individual
independence through education and famously opened with the phrase "Heaven
does not create one person above or below another." Fukuzawa also founded the
newspaper Jiji Shinpō in 1882.

Fukuzawa's later essay Datsu-A Ron ("On Leaving Asia", 1885) advocated that
Japan should align itself culturally and politically with the industrialized
West rather than with its Asian neighbors, a stance that remains
controversial for its role in later Japanese imperialism. His portrait has
appeared on the 10,000-yen banknote since 1984.
